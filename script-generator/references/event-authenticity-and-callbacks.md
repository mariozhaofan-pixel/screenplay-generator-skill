# Event Authenticity and Callback Rules

Use this reference before dialogue polish whenever a screenplay creates or revises a major relationship, plot, reveal, reunion, rupture, victory, or callback event. This file owns event-authenticity tests, evidence hierarchy, prop causality, event identity, and behavior callbacks.

## Event-First Gate

Do not repair a weak event by making its dialogue smoother. For every key event, record:

`intended change | initiating character | voluntary choice | obstacle | other person's visible response | new relationship/information state`

Pass the event only when the change follows from visible choices and responses. If the beat depends on an arbitrary accident, forced misunderstanding, author-arranged rescue, or a third party doing the emotional work, redesign the event before writing final dialogue or CUTs.

Ask in this order:

1. Who actively chooses something that changes the situation?
2. What would remain if the convenient coincidence disappeared?
3. Does the other important character visibly accept, reject, redirect, or complicate the choice?
4. Does the end state differ because of the characters, not only because of an external interruption?
5. Could an ordinary social or professional behavior preserve the same change, preserve character agency, and require fewer arbitrary assumptions?

## Coincidence, Humiliation, and Proxy Tests

- Coincidence may create contact or pressure, but it must not complete the decisive relationship or plot change.
- Do not make one character fall, malfunction, lose clothing, suffer a body/wardrobe accident, or become publicly incompetent merely to make another character attractive or useful.
- Embarrassment is allowed when it follows from character behavior and creates a real consequence; it is not a default bonding engine.
- A helper, friend, superior, message, playback, or bystander must not perform the central apology, invitation, recognition, or relationship decision for the main characters.
- Rescue is valid when danger and competence belong to the premise. Do not add danger solely to manufacture intimacy.
- Remove replay, explanatory flashback, or future promise when the present-tense choice and response already carry the change.

## Relationship and First-Meeting Events

For romance, friendship, partnership, mentorship, and reconciliation, prefer:

`see the other person's real situation -> choose a response -> receive a visible answer -> enter a changed relationship state`

A first meeting does not default to collision, fall, costume failure, mistaken identity, public rescue, or instant destiny. Use those devices only when the current genre and causal design make them necessary.

Attraction or trust should emerge from conduct under real conditions: attention, timing, boundaries, work habits, humor, generosity, refusal, competence, or the choice to stay or leave. The camera may emphasize the response but cannot invent reciprocity that the event does not contain.

## Visible Evidence and Prop Burden

Prefer evidence in this order:

1. character choice
2. response speed and quality
3. distance, access, and blocking
4. repeated work or life behavior
5. natural spoken language and silence
6. a necessary real-world object

A plot-bearing prop must satisfy all three:

- It has a credible practical function in the scene.
- It changes what a character can or must do.
- A character choice or ordinary behavior cannot express the same change with equal causal clarity and fewer arbitrary assumptions.

This test does not ban environmental detail, continuity props, genre artifacts, or a user-requested motif. It prevents a prop from replacing agency. "Make it visual" means make behavior, space, consequence, and attention visible; it does not mean add an object symbol.

## Evidence Provenance, Knowledge Legality, and Hidden-Object Physics

For every clue, recording, document, key, hidden object, or other item that
enables a decisive inference, keep this chain:

`origin/creator -> insertion or availability -> custody/access -> first visible setup -> reveal trigger -> character's permitted inference -> choice changed -> payoff`

- A character may state only what prior experience, disclosed information, or
  currently visible/audible evidence allows. Distinguish observation,
  inference, and confirmation; do not let author knowledge enter dialogue.
- Establish who created or placed a clue, how it reached the location, who
  retained it, and why the current character can access it before its payoff.
- A target object must be selected by an already visible rule. Do not let a
  character open the correct container merely because the plot needs its
  contents.
- For sealed retail, food, medicine, tamper-evident, or controlled goods,
  establish a credible insertion and identification mechanism: controlled
  non-sale stock, visible tamper evidence, a known resealing process, a unique
  mark, or another physically testable distinction. If that cannot be made
  credible, use a controlled container, locker, envelope, drop point, or other
  realistic carrier instead.
- If explaining provenance, custody, concealment, and identification requires
  more arbitrary assumptions than the reveal is worth, redesign the mechanism.

## Brief-Material Classification

Before drafting, classify supplied fragments:

| Class | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Must appear | A required fact, event, line, asset, or constraint |
| Subtext/inspiration | May shape emotion, rhythm, montage, or character strategy without appearing literally |
| Excluded | Must not appear or must remain a post-production/reference layer |

Do not automatically turn a personal anecdote, screenshot, lyric, audio path, or mood fragment into plot fact, voice-over, screen content, or a prop.

## Event Identity and Callback Ledger

Use this ledger when events repeat, rhyme, or may be confused:

`EVT-ID | time/year | project/location | participants | initiating choice | immediate result | later changed meaning | callback ID`

- Give similar activities distinct time, project, location, maturity, or semantic labels.
- A callback repeats a class of choice under changed conditions; it is not merely the same line, pose, or object.
- Record what meaning changed between the original event and callback.
- Link every callback to an established EVT-ID and verify that the later beat is legible without an explanatory speech.
- Do not assign EVT IDs to routine actions that never recur or affect interpretation.

## Silent-Picture and No-Title Checks

For a decisive event:

- Mute dialogue and verify that action, distance, timing, and response still reveal the basic change.
- Remove theme cards, lyrics, and explanatory subtitles and verify that the ending remains understandable.
- Restore dialogue and text only when they add strategy, texture, precision, or a second layer rather than supplying missing causality.

If the event fails either check, repair event choice, response, blocking, or consequence before polishing language.
