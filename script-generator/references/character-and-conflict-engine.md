# Character and Conflict Engine

Use this reference selectively. It is a set of modules, not a mandatory formula. A quiet relationship scene may need only the character module; a disaster story may need an opposing-force module without a human antagonist; a procedural may need the strategy ledger without a power system.

## Route Before Building

Answer these questions internally:

1. Is character identity or change central? If yes, use the character kernel.
2. Does the premise explicitly include a system, power, special access, windfall, or structural advantage? If yes, use the advantage contract. Otherwise skip it.
3. What blocks the protagonist: another goal-driven person, an ideology, coercion, a mirror rival, an impersonal threat, an institution, time, nature, or the protagonist's own pattern? Do not invent a villain when the existing pressure is sufficient.
4. Does the audience need to follow competing plans? If yes, use the information ledger. Otherwise keep the conflict legible and direct.
5. What scale and tone of payoff fits the genre? A restrained drama can resolve through one boundary or changed relationship; spectacle is not the default.

## Character Kernel

Build only the fields that affect scenes:

`role | visible objective | public strategy/mask | hidden need or fear | value boundary | embodied anchor | costly proof choice | state after the choice`

- Give the protagonist at least one decision that changes the problem rather than merely answering pressure.
- Run the replaceability test: if a generic competent person could make the same choices and reach the same result, connect the solution to this character's history, expertise, relationship, flaw, or value.
- Reveal a mask by contradiction under pressure. Let an action violate the character's stated self-image and expose the deeper priority; do not explain the change with a monologue.
- Externalize history through one filmable physical anchor: posture, scar, repeated action, handling of space, prop, or routine. Make it alter behavior or blocking.
- Give important allies an independent objective, useful competence, red line, and exit option. Mutual trust does not require automatic agreement.
- Preserve moral accounting. A late apology, hidden motive, or sympathetic wound may reframe behavior but does not erase damage or consequence.

## Advantage Contract

Use for supernatural powers, systems, wealth/access advantages, privileged information, legal authority, special technology, or any mechanism that can solve problems too easily.

Define the applicable fields:

`capability | access/trigger | hard limit | immediate cost | compounding debt | optional hidden agenda | counterplay | visible consequence`

- Every advantage needs a structural limit, credible counterplay, and a consequence. Use both immediate cost and compounding debt when its story-solving leverage would otherwise flatten escalation; do not invent supernatural payment mechanics for ordinary access or authority.
- Make the protagonist interpret, combine, time, or refuse the advantage. It must not complete the story automatically.
- Let every meaningful use alter a later choice, relationship, resource, body, reputation, or obligation.
- Use uncertainty to create risk before a choice. Randomness may create the problem or change available options; it must not provide the decisive unearned solution.
- If the advantage follows an absolute rule, state enough of that rule for the audience to predict possibilities. Give opponents a way to test, constrain, redirect, or exploit it.
- Plant evidence before revealing a hidden agenda. A reveal should reorganize earlier details instead of adding a new rule at the finish.

## Opposing-Force Selector

Choose one primary function. Add at most one secondary function unless the format has room to develop both.

| Function | Build | Primary dramatic question |
|---|---|---|
| Goal competitor | Incompatible objective, resources, constraints, adaptive plan | Who can secure the objective and at what cost? |
| Ideological rival | Self-consistent values, supporting evidence, unacceptable method | Which value system survives contact with consequences? |
| Corruptor | Leverage that makes success require moral compromise | What will the protagonist surrender to win? |
| Mirror rival | Shared wound or starting point, divergent defining choice | Who might the protagonist have become? |
| Disaster/pressure force | Stable operating pattern, warning signals, boundary, survival response | How can people adapt when negotiation is impossible? |
| Apparent/reframed opponent | Misread motive, planted anomalies, reveal, retained accountability | What changes when the same acts acquire new context? |

For a goal-driven opponent, write:

`objective | why they believe it is justified | current information | resources | constraints | opening plan | adaptation after failure | blind spot | price they will not pay`

- Treat the opponent as the protagonist of their own causal line.
- Let them use a credible strong option based on what they know. Avoid gratuitous provocation, serial weak attacks, forgotten resources, or knowledge they could not possess.
- Competence is not omniscience. Show uncertainty, wrong models, tradeoffs, and strategy updates.
- For an ideological rival, let actions and outcomes test the belief. Dialogue may state the claim, but a speech cannot prove it.
- For a disaster force, opacity does not permit arbitrary behavior. Preserve its pattern and limits.
- For a reframed opponent, the reveal can explain motive but cannot cancel victims, harm, or responsibility.

## Fair Strategy Ledger

First record the information split:

`protagonist knows | opponent knows | audience knows | each side falsely believes | each side still needs`

Then track every strategic turn:

`beat | actor | available facts | objective | usable resource | constraint | chosen action | paid cost | new information | next update`

- The action must be plausible from the actor's current knowledge, not from the writer's full outline.
- A reversal needs prior access to its clue, expertise, prop, relationship, or resource.
- Prefer a third option that changes the objective or frame when it follows established rules. Do not confuse rule-aware reframing with consequence-free cheating.
- Turn an opponent's strength into a blind spot only when the strength has created a repeatable habit, incentive, model, or dependency.
- Professional expertise becomes strategy through specific application and cross-context transfer, not by announcing that a character is brilliant.
- After each result, let capable actors update. Repeating a failed tactic without new reasons is lowered intelligence.
- Defeat can be physical, material, informational, relational, institutional, or ideological. Choose the dimension that answers the story's central conflict.

## Dialogue as Action

For high-stakes dialogue, note:

`surface line | hidden intent | withheld information | leverage | physical action | status before/after`

- Let answers, evasions, counterquestions, silence, and prop handling change who controls the exchange.
- Cut lines that only transfer facts both characters already know.
- Keep the subtext readable through context and behavior; obscurity by itself is not depth.

## Pressure and Payoff

Track what the story has made the protagonist or audience wait for:

`blocked need | source of pressure | accumulated cost | premature release to avoid | countermove | pressure source removed or changed | value revealed | aftermath`

- Build pressure from causal restrictions, not arbitrary humiliation or repeated suffering.
- Delay the decisive answer long enough to create anticipation, but allow intermediate choices and partial changes.
- Make the countermove attack the mechanism supporting the pressure: information, incentive, legitimacy, resource control, relationship, or rule.
- Scale the release to the genre and the accumulated cost. A boundary quietly enforced can be a stronger payoff than domination.
- End with aftermath. Show the new order, debt, wound, relationship, or future pressure created by the win.

## Research Boundary

These rules are an original synthesis developed from authorized study of public screenwriting-craft materials. Keep only abstract mechanisms in generated work. Do not reproduce source transcripts, named examples, slogans, or commercial claims unless the user independently supplies and requests them.
